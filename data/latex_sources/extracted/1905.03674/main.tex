\title{Proportionally Fair Clustering}
\author{Xingyu Chen \qquad Brandon Fain\thanks{corresponding author: btfain at cs.duke.edu} \qquad Liang Lyu \qquad Kamesh Munagala \\ Department of Computer Science, Duke University}
\date{}

\maketitle
%--------------------------------------------------------%

\begin{abstract}We extend the fair machine learning literature by considering the problem of proportional centroid clustering in a metric context. For clustering $n$ points with $k$ centers, we define fairness as proportionality to mean that any $n/k$ points are entitled to form their own cluster if there is another center that is closer in distance for all $n/k$ points. We seek clustering solutions to which there are no such justified complaints from any subsets of agents, without assuming any a priori notion of protected subsets. We present and analyze algorithms to efficiently compute, optimize, and audit proportional solutions. We conclude with an empirical examination of the tradeoff between proportional solutions and the $k$-means objective. \end{abstract}

%\begin{abstract}We extend the fair machine learning literature by considering the problem of proportional centroid clustering in a metric context. We allow that sufficiently large groups of points are entitled to form their own cluster if they can do so to their mutual advantage, and we seek clustering solutions to which there are no such justified complaints. Our notion of entitlement is for all groups of sufficient size, and thus our solution concept has the advantage of not requiring the a priori definition of protected groups. In addition to the introduction this notion of fairness as proportionality, we contribute algorithms to efficiently audit and compute proportional solutions, and to optimize a global objective subject to proportionality. We conclude with an empirical examination of the tradeoff between proportional solutions and the celebrated $k$-means algorithm. \end{abstract}

%Exact proportional clusterings are not always guaranteed to exist, but we show that we can efficiently compute provably constant approximate proportional clusterings. Additionally, we consider the problem where approximate proportionality is imposed as a constraint to the $k$-medians or $k$-means objective, and we give a constant factor approximation algorithm that runs in polynomial time. We further demonstrate that given a clustering, we can sample to efficiently check for a violation of proportionality, without needing to compute all pairwise distances. Finally, we give a local search heuristic that we implement in our experiments, where we demonstrate that traditional clustering algorithms like $k$-means do indeed sometimes produce very non-proportional clusterings, but that our algorithms can correct this while maintaining reasonable approximations on the $k$-means objective. \end{abstract}
