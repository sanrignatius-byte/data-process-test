\begin{abstract}
We study the fair variant of the classic $k$-median problem introduced by~\cite{chierichetti2017fair}. In the standard $k$-median problem, given an input pointset $P$, the goal is to find $k$ centers $C$ and assign each input point to one of the centers in $C$ such that the average distance of points to their cluster center is minimized.
 In the fair variant of $k$-median, the points are colored, and the goal is to minimize the same average distance objective while ensuring that all clusters have an ``approximately equal'' number of points of each color. 

\cite{chierichetti2017fair} proposed a two-phase algorithm for fair $k$-clustering. In the first step, the pointset is partitioned into subsets called {\em fairlets} that satisfy the fairness requirement and approximately preserve the $k$-median objective. In the second step,  fairlets are merged into $k$ clusters by one of the existing $k$-median algorithms. The running time of this algorithm is dominated by the first step, which takes super-quadratic time. 

In this paper, we present a practical approximate fairlet decomposition algorithm that runs in {\em nearly linear} time. Our algorithm additionally allows for finer control over the balance of resulting clusters than the original work. We complement our theoretical bounds with empirical evaluation.
\end{abstract}
